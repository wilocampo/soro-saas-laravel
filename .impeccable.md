# Impeccable — Design Guidelines for Soro

## Design Context

### Users
Philippine SME owners, their in-house bookkeepers, external accountants, and
auditors. They are keeping real double-entry books that get filed with the
BIR, often under deadline. Two distinct competence levels share the same
screens: a non-accountant business owner who needs the tool to be legible,
and a professional accountant who needs it to be exact and fast for
high-volume document entry. The context is work, not leisure — this is a
daily-driver system of record, not something used for pleasure.

The job to be done: record transactions correctly, see where the money is,
and produce books and returns that survive a BIR examination.

### Brand Personality
**Trustworthy · precise · quiet.** The product's whole reason to exist is
correctness you can stake a tax filing on — the interface must earn that
trust visually. It should feel like a well-set financial statement:
exact, auditable, and calm. Never loud, never playful, never "fun money."
The voice is plain and direct (a control says exactly what it does; an error
says what went wrong and how to fix it), and it never dresses up uncertainty
as fact — the same honesty the ledger enforces on the data.

Emotional goal: **confidence.** A user should feel the numbers are safe here.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **System:** Sakai PrimeVue 4 / Aura preset / Tailwind v3, dark mode via
  `.app-dark`. This is fixed and non-negotiable — `docs/specs/11-frontend-components.md`
  is the law. Impeccable fills gaps within that system; it never replaces it.
- **Accent:** emerald (the Aura `emerald` primary). Green earns its place in a
  money product; it is the single accent and is not spent on decoration.
- **Surfaces:** cards separate from the page by a **soft elevation shadow**
  (no heavy borders) — a restrained lift, not a floating consumer-app card.
- **Both themes** are first-class and must come free from theme tokens, never
  from `isDark ?` ternaries or hardcoded hex.
- **Reference feel:** a clean bank statement or a QuickBooks-grade ledger
  screen — information-dense but unhurried. **Anti-reference:** crypto/fintech
  dashboards with neon gradients, oversized hero numbers, celebratory
  micro-animations, or emoji. Money here is serious.
- **Numbers are the hero.** Amounts are the most important content on almost
  every screen: tabular-nums, right-aligned in columns, centavos formatted
  through `MoneyText`, negatives in red-severity. Typography and spacing serve
  the readability of figures first.

### Design Principles
1. **Correctness is the aesthetic.** The visual design should reinforce that
   the numbers are trustworthy — alignment, precision, and restraint over
   flourish. If a choice makes the page prettier but the figures harder to
   trust or scan, it is the wrong choice.
2. **Theme tokens only, never hardcoded color.** No raw hex, no
   `bg-green-100 text-green-800` badges, no `isDark ?` ternaries. Use
   `severity`/`Tag`, surface tokens, and `var(--p-*)`. Dark mode must be free.
   (Spec 11 rule 2.)
3. **`.card` is the one container**, and every card separates from the ground
   the same way (soft shadow). No hand-rolled `bg-white dark:bg-gray-800`
   blocks.
4. **Quiet by default; loud only for what needs attention.** Semantic color
   (danger/warn/success) is reserved for state that genuinely needs the eye —
   overdue balances, out-of-balance, failed checks. Everything else stays in
   neutral surface tokens. Never decorate with color.
5. **Scan before read.** These are operated screens, not documents: surface
   the summary before the detail, encode state in form (a `StatusTag`, a
   severity pill) as well as in number, and keep money columns aligned so a
   figure reads at a glance.
